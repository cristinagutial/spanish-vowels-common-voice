### The balanced design closes the gap

Source: `s6_naive_reproduction.csv`. On the new sample the most prolific Sur

them to disagree about. That convergence is the argument for the per-speaker cap.

**Vowel space area** (convex hull, Lobanov): Norte 3.05, Sur 2.95, *d* = +0.12,
95 % CI **[−0.05, +0.28]**, *p* = 0.167. The interval now excludes anything above
a small effect.

### Mixed models on speaker × vowel means

All dialect main effects n.s. (`s6_mixed_models_speaker_means.csv`).

**A methodological trap worth writing up.** Token-level mixed models on Lobanov
values report ICC ≈ 0 and *p* < 0.001. That is arithmetic, not evidence: Lobanov
sets every speaker's mean to 0 and SD to 1, leaving no between-speaker variance
for a random intercept to absorb, so the model reverts to treating tokens as
independent. A random intercept protects against speakers differing in *level*,
not against one speaker shaping a *within-speaker pattern*. Aggregating to
speaker × vowel cells first is the fix.

### Power

186 Sur vs 461 Norte speakers ⇒ 80 % power for *d* ≥ **0.24**; 90 % power for
*d* ≥ 0.28. (The midterm-sized sample could only resolve *d* ≥ 0.63.)

## 4. Outgroup calibration — is the method capable of detecting a dialect?

Source: `s6_outgroup_calibration.csv`, `fig_outgroup.png`. Speaker-level, across
12 vowel measures.

| contrast | mean \|*d*\| | max \|*d*\| | significant |
|----------|--------------|-------------|-------------|
| Norte vs Sur | **0.064** | 0.172 | **0 / 12** |
| Norte vs México | 0.152 | 0.303 | 4 / 12 |
| Sur vs México | 0.173 | 0.411 | 2 / 12 |

Iberian/Mexican contrasts average **2.5×** the Norte/Sur effect and reach
significance; Norte/Sur reaches it nowhere. Same measurements, same pipeline,
same tests.

Largest individual effects: Sur vs México F2 /a/ *d* = −0.41, F2 /i/ *d* = +0.40;
Norte vs México F2 /i/ *d* = +0.30, duration *d* = −0.28.

**Note for the discussion.** Even the transatlantic contrast is small in absolute
terms (mean |*d*| ≈ 0.16). The five-vowel Spanish system is famously stable, and
its dialectal variation is overwhelmingly *consonantal*. A null result in the
vowel space is therefore the theoretically expected outcome, not a surprise — and
§5 is where the variation actually lives.

## 5. Coda /s/ — the positive finding

Source: `s10_*.csv`, `fig_codas.png`. 21,639 /s/ tokens (12,269 coda, 9,370
onset), 890 speakers.

### The /s/ itself — token-level means

| variety | position | CoG (Hz) | duration (s) | n |
|---------|----------|----------|--------------|---|
| Norte | coda | 3065 | 0.100 | 5,821 |
| Norte | onset | 3466 | 0.099 | 4,179 |
| México | coda | 2801 | 0.100 | 3,893 |
| México | onset | 3652 | 0.102 | 3,215 |
| **Sur** | **coda** | **2419** | **0.090** | 2,555 |
| Sur | onset | 3377 | 0.102 | 1,976 |

### Weakening index (onset CoG − coda CoG; higher = more coda weakening)

| variety | index | speakers |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Norte | +359 Hz | 369 |
| México | +836 Hz | 249 |
| **Sur** | **+1022 Hz** | 165 |

**Sur vs (Norte + México): *d* = +0.63, *p* < 0.0001.**

The ordering matches the dialectological literature: Northern Peninsular Spanish
is the most conservative for coda /s/, Andalusian the most innovative, Mexican
intermediate (coastal varieties aspirate, highland varieties do not).

### Speaker-level Norte vs Sur, by position

| position | measure | Norte | Sur | *d* | *p*(Holm) |
|----------|---------|-------|-----|-----|-----------|
| **coda** | CoG | 3118.7 | 2417.1 | **+0.56** | **< 0.001** |
| **coda** | duration | — | — | **+0.30** | **0.019** |
| coda | RMS | — | — | +0.07 | 1.000 |
| onset | CoG | 3517.0 | 3450.1 | **+0.06** | **1.000** |
| onset | duration | — | — | −0.27 | 0.033 |
| onset | RMS | — | — | +0.03 | 1.000 |

The contrast appears in codas and vanishes in onsets — the within-speaker control
that distinguishes a phonological process from a general difference in sibilant
production.

**Dialect × position interaction on CoG: +196.9 Hz, *p* = 0.006.**

### The preceding vowel

815 speakers contribute both contexts (397 Norte, 168 Sur, 250 México).

| measure | dialect × context | *p*(Holm) |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|
| F1 Lobanov | +0.101 | **0.005** |
| F2 Lobanov | +0.070 | **0.036** |
| Duration | +0.003 | **0.036** |

Within-speaker shift from other contexts to pre-coda /s/:

| measure | Norte | Sur | *d* | *p* |
|---------|-------|-----|-----|-----|
| F1 (Lobanov z) | −0.146 (n=397) | −0.086 (n=168) | −0.17 | 0.063 |
| Duration | −0.0016 | −0.0002 | −0.10 | 0.241 |

**Report this carefully.** The interaction is significant, but the effect is
small, and the direction needs stating precisely: *both* varieties close the
vowel before coda /s/; Sur simply closes it **less**. This is not the dramatic
"Southern vowels open" of the descriptive literature.

The earlier 34-speaker sample gave *d* = −0.83 for this contrast; at 168 Sur
speakers it is −0.17. That shrinkage is itself worth a paragraph — it is regression
to the mean, and a good illustration of why small samples produce large effects.

## 6. Clustering

Source: `s7_*.csv`, `fig_clustering.png`. ARI against true vowel labels, k = 5:

| features | dims | ARI | silhouette |
|----------|------|-----|------------|
| F1/F2 Lobanov | 2 | **0.424** | 0.361 |
| F1/F2 + duration | 3 | 0.397 | 0.301 |
| F1/F2/B1/B2 Lobanov | 4 | 0.357 | 0.303 |
| F1/F2 raw Hz | 2 | 0.332 | 0.352 |
| F1/F2/B1/B2 raw Hz | 4 | 0.258 | 0.273 |

Normalisation improves recovery (0.424 vs 0.332); bandwidth degrades it.

**Choosing k** (F1/F2 Lobanov): silhouette peaks at **k = 3** (0.454), ARI peaks
at k = 4 (0.479). True number of vowels is 5.

| k | silhouette | ARI |
|---|-----------|-----|
| 2 | 0.407 | 0.282 |
| **3** | **0.454** | 0.433 |
| **4** | 0.418 | **0.479** |
| 5 | 0.361 | 0.424 |
| 6 | 0.343 | 0.400 |

This reproduces the midterm's k = 3 exactly and shows it was a property of the
metric, not evidence for "three perceptual zones of Spanish". Silhouette rewards
few compact blobs.

**Bandwidth:** adding B1/B2 changes silhouette by −0.058 and ARI by −0.067. The
midterm's inference ("silhouette drops, therefore bandwidth captures different
structure") was invalid, because silhouette falls with added dimensions
regardless of cluster quality. ARI is comparable across dimensionalities;
silhouette is not.

### Predicting dialect, Norte vs Sur (chance = 0.500)

| model | random folds | speaker-disjoint folds | inflation |
|-------|--------------|------------------------|-----------|
| LDA | 0.500 | 0.500 | +0.000 |
| Logistic | 0.500 | 0.500 | +0.000 |
| SVM (RBF) | 0.500 | 0.500 | +0.000 |
| Random forest | 0.502 | 0.498 | +0.004 |

### Pairwise, speaker-disjoint

| contrast | best accuracy | model | speakers |
|----------|---------------|-------|----------|
| Norte vs Sur | 0.500 | LDA | 624 |
| Norte vs México | 0.527 | Random forest | 725 |
| Sur vs México | 0.530 | Random forest | 463 |

### Predicting vowel identity — positive control (chance = 0.200)

| model | random folds | speaker-disjoint folds |
|-------|--------------|------------------------|
| LDA | 0.719 | 0.719 |
| Logistic | 0.728 | 0.727 |
| SVM (RBF) | 0.755 | 0.756 |
| Random forest | 0.759 | **0.762** |

Vowels are recovered at **3.8× chance** across unseen speakers; dialect sits
exactly at chance.

**A second demonstration of the sampling point.** In the midterm-style sample,
random folds inflated dialect accuracy by **+0.20** over speaker-disjoint folds.
Here the inflation is **+0.004**. Speaker leakage only inflates results when a few
speakers dominate; balance the design and the two validation schemes converge.

## 8. Measurement validity

Source: `tests/test_pipeline.py` (24 tests, all passing).

Synthetic vowels with known formants (full F1–F5 resonator bank, F0 = 120 Hz):

| vowel | true F1 | measured | error | true F2 | measured | error |
|-------|---------|----------|-------|---------|----------|-------|
| i | 300 | 340 | +13.5 % | 2200 | 2130 | −3.2 % |
| e | 450 | 479 | +6.5 % | 1900 | 1885 | −0.8 % |
| a | 700 | 701 | **+0.2 %** | 1400 | 1444 | +3.2 % |
| o | 470 | 508 | +8.2 % | 1000 | 1102 | +10.2 % |
| u | 320 | 428 | **+33.7 %** | 800 | 912 | +14.0 % |

Linear prediction overestimates F1 for close vowels: F1 approaches F0 so
individual harmonics dominate the fitted envelope, and for /u/ the F1 and F2 poles
interfere. Accurate for open vowels. The bias is identical across dialects, so it
cannot manufacture a difference, but /u/ is the least trustworthy measurement.

## 10. Claims the report can and cannot make

**Supported**
- The midterm's F1/F2/B2 effects were artifacts of clip-level sampling; the
  mechanism is documented, quantified, and reproduced.
- No detectable Norte/Sur difference in the vowel space, at 461 vs 186 speakers,
  with everything detectable above *d* = 0.24 ruled out.
- The method does detect the Iberian/Mexican contrast, so the null is about the
  dialects rather than the instrument.
- **Southern speakers weaken coda /s/**: *d* = +0.56 against Norte, *d* = +0.63
  against Norte and México pooled, with onset /s/ as a null control and a
  significant dialect × position interaction.
- A small but significant vowel effect before coda /s/ (both varieties close the
  vowel; Sur closes it less).
- k = 3 in the midterm's clustering was a property of silhouette, not of Spanish.
- Bandwidth degrades vowel-category recovery.
- Features are valid: vowels classified at 3.8× chance across unseen speakers.

**Not supported**
- "Norte and Sur have identical vowel spaces." Differences below *d* = 0.24 remain
  possible.
- Compensatory vowel *opening* in the strong form claimed by the descriptive
  literature — the interaction is significant but small, and both varieties close.
- Compensatory *lengthening* before coda /s/ — tested, not found.
- Any claim resting on /u/, given the +34 % F1 measurement bias.
- Generalisation beyond read speech by Common Voice volunteers with self-reported
  dialect labels.
